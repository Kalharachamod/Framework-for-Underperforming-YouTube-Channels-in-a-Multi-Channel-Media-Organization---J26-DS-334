# Component 3: Literature Gap Matrix

*Diffusion-Based Cross-Channel Audience Bridge Scoring* (STEP 32, research positioning)

This matrix compares existing approaches with what Component 3 does. A limitation is listed only where the cited work's own description supports it. Where we have only read an abstract or a search summary, the entry says **(abstract-level)** and must be checked against the full text before it goes into the thesis. Full references are in [component3_research_position.md](component3_research_position.md#references).

| Existing approach | What it does | Limitation (for *this* research question) | Component 3 response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commenter overlap between channels** (Ribeiro et al. 2020 [R1]; Violot et al. 2025 [R2]; Trujillo et al. 2020 [R3]) | Measures how many commenters two channels share, usually with Jaccard similarity or an overlap coefficient, then studies the resulting channel network. | Overlap is a **pair statistic**. It is symmetric (Jaccard) and is not combined with content similarity or multi-hop graph structure. These studies aim to *describe* communities (political, radicalisation, fringe platforms), not to *rank* candidate partner channels for one source. | Keeps shared-commenter evidence, but uses it as a **confidence factor** (`n/(n+k)`) rather than the score itself. Adds multi-hop diffusion, content similarity and learned graph similarity, and ranks destinations **per source**. |
| **Cross-channel / bipartite commenter network projections** (Violot et al. 2025 [R2]; Shajari et al. 2023 [R4, R5]) | Builds channel-commenter or video-commenter bipartite graphs and projects them onto channels (or commenters). Network statistics, Graph2Vec or clustering are then used for description or anomaly detection. | **Homogeneous projections.** A projection discards the video and topic layers, and the work targets description or coordinated-behaviour detection rather than audience-bridge ranking. | Keeps a **heterogeneous** commenter–video–channel–topic graph (no projection), so diffusion and embeddings can use paths through videos and topics. |
| **Community detection on overlap networks** (Louvain: Blondel et al. 2008 [F5]; applied to YouTube overlap networks, e.g. the German far-right study [R6], abstract-level) | Partitions channels into communities of densely shared audiences. | Communities are **unordered and symmetric**: two channels are either in the same community or not. There is no graded, directional ranking of *which* channel is the strongest bridge from a given source. | Louvain is implemented **as a baseline** (STEP 20) and compared with the proposed ranking in STEP 21 (agreement, top-k overlap). |
| **Directed / migration-style audience relationships** (Ribeiro et al. 2020 [R1]: users migrating over time from milder to more extreme channels; Violot et al. 2025 [R2]: the share of one group's commenters also active in another group, which is asymmetric) | Shows that audience flow or overlap can be **asymmetric**. Ribeiro et al. do this temporally (who commented where first); Violot et al. do it through a source-conditioned percentage. | Direction is studied between **channel groups / ideological categories**, as a descriptive finding. Neither builds a per-channel directed **ranking score** combining several signals. (Ribeiro: abstract-level for the exact migration method.) | Directional by design: source-personalised PageRank, plus **per-source** normalisation and ranking. Component 3 does **not** claim directional audience relationships are new, and does not claim migration. |
| **Graph representation learning on platform data** (node2vec [F4]; metapath2vec [F2]; HGT [F3]; HIN recommendation such as HERec [R8]; Tube2Vec [R7] applies node2vec to a YouTube channel graph) | Learns vector representations of nodes; similar vectors mean structurally similar nodes. Tube2Vec builds channel embeddings from recommendation, sharing and metadata signals. | Embedding similarity is **symmetric** (cosine) and its meaning is opaque. In Tube2Vec the graph is built from recommendations or Reddit sharing, not from **commenter co-activity**, and the output is an embedding, not a bridge ranking (abstract-level). | Uses metapath2vec (primary) and HGT (alternative) as **one additive component** of the score, so their contribution can be seen and ablated. node2vec is kept **as a baseline**. |
| **Semantic / content similarity of channels** (sentence/text embeddings; Tube2Vec's semantic channel embeddings [R7]; multilingual E5 [F7]) | Compares what channels publish (titles, descriptions, metadata). | Content similarity says nothing about **shared audience**: two channels can publish similar content to disjoint audiences. | Used as one component (topic similarity), **gated by evidence**. A pair with no shared commenters gets confidence 0 and score 0, however similar the content. Topic edges are excluded from PPR by default to avoid double counting. |
| **Diffusion / random-walk proximity** (PageRank [F1]; topic-sensitive / Personalized PageRank, Haveliwala 2002 [F6]; PPR in recommendation, e.g. Twitter WTF [R14], and PathRank on heterogeneous graphs [R9]) | Scores how reachable each node is from a source by random walks with restart. It is widely used for personalised recommendation and link prediction. | PPR is a **general-purpose proximity measure**. We found no work applying it on a commenter–video–channel–topic graph to rank cross-channel audience bridges, but this is an absence-of-evidence finding (see risks). | Uses PPR from each channel as the **diffusion** component (multi-hop audience reachability), min-max normalised per source. |
| **Confidence / evidence (support) weighting** (significance weighting, Herlocker et al. 1999 [R10]; shrunk similarity `n/(n+λ)·ρ`, Bell & Koren 2007 [R11]) | Down-weights similarities that rest on few co-observations. | **Established concept.** The exact form `n/(n+k)` is essentially the Bell & Koren shrinkage factor. | Applies the established shrinkage form to **shared commenters**, multiplied by **topic-profile coverage**. The specific combination for audience-bridge scoring is the proposed element; the shrinkage idea itself is not new. |

## Summary of the gap (one line)

Existing work measures commenter overlap, detects communities, learns channel embeddings and computes graph proximity **separately**, mostly to describe ideological or anomalous communities. We did not find work that **combines heterogeneous-graph diffusion, learned graph similarity, content similarity and evidence weighting into a directional, per-source ranking of candidate audience bridges between channels**. That integration, not any single technique, is the potential contribution. It still needs validation through a fuller literature review (see [component3_novelty_threat_analysis.md](component3_novelty_threat_analysis.md)).
